Upagraha Satellite Team · Thermal Subsystem Lead · 2023–2025

Passive thermal control for a 3U CubeSat

Thermal mapping of a 3U CubeSat in a 500 km polar sun-synchronous orbit (97.5° inclination, 94-minute period). I set up the orbit in STK to get the beta angle and sun geometry, then built the boundary conditions (solar flux, albedo, Earth IR, internal heat) for a meshed SolidWorks model in ANSYS Transient Thermal. I ran it through sunlit and eclipse phases for three operating modes: maximum power, payload/data transmission and contingency.

Avg. max temperature · sunlit, max-power mode

Bare structure123 °C
No MLI108 °C
With MLI48 °C

Result MLI brought the peak sunlit temperature down from about 108 °C to 48 °C and kept all components within their operating ranges. The cold eclipse case still needs a resistive heater, positioned for the payload and batteries.

ANSYS Transient Thermal · STK (AGI) · SolidWorks · MLI · TVAC / thermal-balance test planning

Research

Journal manuscript

Aerodynamic and Aeroacoustic Performance of Parabolic and Sinusoidal Leading-Edge Tubercles on a NACA 0020 Airfoil

Sudarshan B, Jayanth VT, Vikhram S, Vignesh P, Sohan CS, Santhosh S Somshekhar, Venkatesh T L · BMS College of Engineering

Tubercle studies usually look at forces, flow structure or noise on their own. This paper measures all three on the same seven NACA 0020 configurations (a baseline plus three parabolic and three sinusoidal amplitudes) at Re ≈ 5×10⁴–1.5×10⁵ and 0–20° angle of attack, and then correlates the results. The main finding is that profile shape, not amplitude alone, sets the trade-off between aerodynamic efficiency and trailing-edge noise.

Force balance · photoluminescent flow visualisation · aeroacoustic measurement · cross-technique correlation

Conference poster · IAES 2024 · Mar 2023 – Apr 2024

Aeroacoustic study on modified airfoils

The project that started my airfoil research. We modified a NACA 0020 with leading-edge tubercles (sinusoidal and parabolic) and trailing-edge serrations, then measured how its self-noise changed in a low-noise wind tunnel at 5 and 10 m/s and 0°, 5° and 15° angle of attack, recording on four planes around the airfoil.

Result The parabolic tubercles were the quietest geometry, cutting noise by about 10 dB at 5° and 10 m/s. The abstract was accepted for a poster at the International Conference on Advances in Aerospace and Energy Systems, hosted by ISRO's Liquid Propulsion Systems Centre.

Sohan CS, Sudarshan B, Jayanth VT, Vikhram S, Vignesh P · Paper ID IAES-2024-675

Low-noise wind tunnel · sound level measurement · trailing-edge serrations · leading-edge tubercles
